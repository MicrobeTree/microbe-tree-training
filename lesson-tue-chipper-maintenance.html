Chipper Maintenance: Sharp, Set, Tight, Greased, Locked Out

Knives, anvil gap, belts, grease and the daily walk-around on our Bandits, and zero energy before anyone opens the hood.

🔧 The short answer

A chipper with sharp knives, the right anvil gap, tight belts and greased bearings feeds itself. A neglected one plugs, vibrates and tempts people to push wood in with their hands and feet. Bandit's own manual says dull knives make an operator "push, kick, or wrestle with the wood", which is unsafe. Maintenance is a safety job.

⚠️ Before you open the hood, touch a knife, or clear a jamBandit's pre-maintenance shutdown, in order: disengage the clutch → wait for the drum to come to a complete stop → engine off → remove the ignition key and keep it in your possession → install the drum lock pin → disconnect the battery. Bandit also says to have at least two people on site during maintenance and service.

🔒 WorkSafeBC Part 10: lockout

  • 10.1: "maintenance" includes cleaning, lubricating and clearing obstructions. So greasing and clearing a plug both count.
  • 10.3(1): when equipment is shut down for maintenance, no work until parts are secured against inadvertent movement (that's the drum lock pin), energy hazards are controlled, and isolating devices are locked out as Part 10 requires.
  • 10.4 & 10.6: personal locks, no combination locks, each lock marked to identify who applied it, and you verify the lockout before starting work.
  • 10.7: you keep immediate control of your key(s) for the whole job.

Follow Microbe's lockout procedure for the chipper. If you're not sure what it says, ask Anton before you open anything.

🔪 Knives: sharpen, rotate, replace

  • Angle: bolt-in knives ground at 30° to 30½°. Machine-ground flat and straight, with coolant. No hand-held power grinder: Bandit says the angle can't be held and heat will distort the metal. No hollow grind.
  • Daily touch-up: Bandit's Knife Saver Kit or a #10 flat bastard mill file can bring an edge back without pulling the knife. This doesn't fix badly nicked knives.
  • Minimum width: never sharpen past the minimum in the manual (the 12XPC drum-knife diagram shows 2 9/16 in / 65.1 mm). Under minimum = discard.
  • Sets: replace knives in sets of the same width to keep the drum balanced and cut vibration.
  • Hardware: factory-approved only, torqued to the chart, no anti-seize on knife bolts. Replace knife hardware after 4–5 knife rotations/changes at most.

📏 Anvil gap

  • Check weekly or whenever knives are changed. Use a feeler gauge on both sides of each knife, then set the anvil to the closest knife.
  • 12XPC: 0.120 in (3.0 mm). Other Bandits are very different: the 1290/1690 manual says 0.028–0.032 in, and the 490XP says 0.045–0.065 in. Use the manual for your machine.
  • The anvil wears. When it's worn, rotate it to a new working edge. Bandit lists a wrong anvil gap as a cause of dull knives.

⚙️ Belts & grease

  • Belts: use the beltshield slot for a quick daily check. Every month, pull the shield and check with a tension tester. 12XPC main drive: 3/8 in (9.5 mm) deflection at 60–64 lb (27.2–29.0 kg).
  • New belts stretch. Adjust after 1 hour of running, then every 4 hours until they stop stretching. Loose belts slip, glaze and are ruined. Never use belt dressing.
  • Grease: purge the chipper bearings daily with EP-2 lithium grease, and give the feedwheel bearings 1 shot. Wipe off the excess, because it attracts dirt.

👀 Daily walk-around (Bandit's list, short version)

  • Decals and gauges · safety equipment, including the last-chance stop and engine disable plug · loose bolts · guards · hood hinge.
  • With everything shut down: open the hood and look for foreign objects. Turn the drum a full revolution with a pry bar or wood bar, never your hands. If it's jammed or frozen, don't start it.
  • Close up: hood pin and padlock, engine disable plug in · hydraulic oil 3/4–7/8 full · leaks (never check for hydraulic leaks with fingers or skin) · tires, lug nuts, hitch.
📋 Field Note: why it plugs

Bandit: plugging is usually from letting the engine drop below full RPM (shut the feedwheels off when it lugs) or from dull knives making chunks and slivers. Rotten material with little substance can plug the chute too. When it does plug: full shutdown, gloved hands and a raking tool, and a bar, never a hand, to turn a jammed drum. It can break free suddenly at the pinch point.

Question 1 of 5
The chipper just plugged. What has to happen before anyone opens the hood?
Zero energy first
  • Bandit 12XPC manual: disengage clutch, wait for the disc/drum to come to a complete stop, turn off engine, remove the ignition key, make sure the ignition key is in your possession, install the disc/drum lock pin, and disconnect the battery.
  • WorkSafeBC 10.1: clearing obstructions counts as maintenance, so the Part 10 lockout rules apply.
Question 2 of 5
You're setting the anvil on a Bandit you haven't used before. What clearance do you use?
Model-specific, every time
  • 12XPC: 0.120 in (3.0 mm) from the highest knife. 1290/1690: 0.028–0.032 in. 490XP: 0.045–0.065 in.
  • Check both sides of each knife with a feeler gauge and set to the closest knife. Do it weekly or whenever knives are changed.

Question 3 of 5
Which knife practice does Bandit say NOT to do?
No angle grinder on chipper knives
  • Bandit: don't use a hand-held power grinder. The knife angle can't be held and heat will distort the metal.
  • Bolt-in knives are ground at 30°–30½°, machine-ground flat, with coolant. Never go past the minimum width.
  • Replace knife hardware after 4–5 rotations/changes at most.
Question 4 of 5
The chipper got new drive belts yesterday. What's the plan?
New belts stretch
  • Bandit: new belts stretch very soon. Adjust after one hour, then every four hours until they quit stretching, or they can burn and fly off.
  • Never use belt dressing. Over- or under-tensioning shortens belt and bearing life.
  • 12XPC main drive spec: 3/8 in deflection at 60–64 lb.
Question 5 of 5
On the daily walk-around, how do you check the drum turns freely?
Bar, not hands
  • Bandit daily start-up #8: very carefully, manually with a pry bar or wood bar, turn the chipper drum a full revolution to confirm anvil and knife clearance. If it's jammed or frozen, do not start the engine and engage the clutch.
  • Pinch points sit between the drum and its housing. A jammed drum can break free suddenly.
