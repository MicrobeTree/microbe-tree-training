📋 Why the Pre-Climb Inspection Exists

ANSI Z133 (Safety Requirements for Arboricultural Operations) §4 mandates that before any arboricultural work begins, a qualified arborist inspect the tree and work site to identify hazards. This is not optional paperwork — it is the mechanism by which crews turn general training into specific decisions for this tree, on this day.

The pre-climb inspection has three functions:

  • Hazard identification — find what could injure you before you're exposed to it
  • Work planning — translate the inspection findings into a sequence of work that controls the hazards
  • Communication — ensure every person on the crew knows the plan, the hazards, and their role (the tailgate meeting)

Most serious arborist injuries occur in situations where a known hazard was not communicated, or where a no-go condition existed but work proceeded anyway. A rigorous pre-climb inspection is not a bureaucratic burden — it is the single most effective tool for bringing everyone home.

🔄 The Inspection Sequence

1. Arrive / read the site from the vehicle (wide view)
2. Ground-level 360° walk-around of the tree
3. Site hazard scan (utilities, DZ, access, ground conditions)
4. Tree structural assessment (crown, root zone, attachment points)
5. Identify tie-in points and rigging anchors
6. Develop work plan and DZ/drop zone layout
7. Tailgate meeting — brief the full crew
8. Go / No-Go decision

Steps 1–7 happen before anyone puts on a harness. The inspection is always done by a qualified line-clearance arborist or ISA Certified Arborist — someone with the training to interpret what they find.

🌲 Tree Structural Assessment Checklist

Complete a full 360° walk-around at minimum. Back up far enough to see the whole crown. Use binoculars if needed. Assess from multiple angles — lean, crown asymmetry, and structural defects are often only visible from one direction.

Root Zone & Base
Root plate heaving or lifting?
One-sided heave on windward side = root plate failure risk; do not climb until assessed
Soil crack around root collar?
Cracking in a semicircle = root plate beginning to overturn; high-risk condition
Basal decay or cavity?
Sound test (mallet), look for conks/brackets, staining, soft wood at base
Girdling roots or soil level changes?
Buried root flare = reduced stability; note for PHC report
Lean angle & direction?
Lean toward DZ = identify before planning cuts; >15° lean in a compromised tree = reassess
Trunk
Bark condition — tight, slipping, loose?
Slipping bark = cambium death; the tree or that section may be structurally compromised
Cankers, wounds, or cracks in trunk?
Large cracks (especially horizontal) can indicate severe internal decay
Included bark at major unions?
Do not tie-in above an included union without a rated cabling system in place
Codominant stems — bark ridge or inclusion?
Strong U-shaped union with bark ridge = use cautiously; V-shaped with included bark = high risk
Crown
Dead wood in crown (hangers, widow-makers)?
Identify all hangers before entering crown; plan removal sequence
Epicormic sprouting pattern?
Dense epicormics on scaffold limbs = internal decay compensating; reduce loading
Crown dieback extent & distribution?
Top-down dieback = root, vascular, or systemic issue; reassess climbing plan
Proposed tie-in points sound and adequate?
Visually inspect from ground; do not TIP into dead, heavily decayed, or compromised branches
Wind — current conditions and forecast?
High wind = defer; gusts exceeding 40 km/h = no-go for crown work on most residential trees

⚡ Site Hazard Identification

Overhead Utilities (Priority 1 — assess before anything else)
Any overhead lines present?
Identify: service drop to building, distribution lines, communication lines — they all need separate assessment
Energized power lines within 3 m of work?
STOP — line-clearance qualified crew required; notify utility company; zone of no entry without de-energization
Service drop insulation intact?
Frayed or damaged insulation = treat as uninsulated; do not touch
Communication (telecom/cable) lines in tree?
Not energized but can be under tension; notify provider if cutting near them
Drop Zone (DZ)
Clear DZ established and marked?
Size DZ conservatively — account for bounce, roll, and unexpected trajectory; 1.5× piece length minimum
Public and bystanders excluded from DZ?
Cones, flagging tape, or barriers; assign a ground person to watch the exclusion zone
Structures, vehicles, or property in potential DZ?
Plan rigging to redirect pieces away from property if DZ is compromised
Ground stability in DZ — soft, sloped, uneven?
Log roll risk on slopes; chip truck positioning on soft ground can shift
Access & Ground Conditions
Underground utilities located (BC 1-call)?
Before any ground-disturbing work; BC 1-Call: 1-800-474-6886; 3 business days minimum
Traffic control required?
Road allowance work or DZ affecting traffic = WCB-compliant traffic control plan
Irrigation lines or hardscape at root zone?
Note for ground crew with shovels; report to client if discovered

🗣️ The Tailgate Meeting — What It Must Cover

ANSI Z133 §4.1 requires that all crew members are informed of hazards and the work plan before operations begin. The tailgate meeting is that communication event. It does not need to be long — 3 to 5 minutes on a typical residential job — but every item below must be covered.

Tailgate Meeting Required Elements
Tree and site hazards identified
Walk the crew to each hazard; don't just describe it — point to it
Work sequence and plan explained
What gets removed first, why, and in what order; how rigging will be set up
DZ boundaries communicated and understood
Where to stand, where NOT to stand; confirm everyone can describe the DZ back to you
Communication signals confirmed
Verbal signals ("TIMBER," "ROPE," "CLEAR"); noise conditions — chainsaw, chipper = hand signals
PPE requirements for this specific job
Chainsaw chaps, face shield, hearing protection, climbing helmet — not generic, specific to today's work
Emergency procedures
Where is the nearest hospital (Kelowna General: 2268 Pandosy); who calls 911; who provides first aid
Roles confirmed
Climber, ground crew, spotter, traffic control — every person knows their assignment
Questions invited and addressed
Crew members have authority to raise concerns; this is the moment to surface them

Rule: If anyone on the crew cannot describe the DZ, the work sequence, and what to do if something goes wrong — the tailgate meeting is not finished.

✅ Go / No-Go Decision Framework

The go/no-go decision is made by the qualified arborist in charge after completing the inspection and tailgate meeting. Work proceeds only when all go conditions are met and all no-go conditions are absent or controlled.

✅ GO Conditions

CategoryCondition
TreeNo uncontrolled structural failure risk; proposed TIP points are sound; hangers identified and planned for
UtilitiesNo energized lines within work zone; or qualified line-clearance crew present and utility notified
DZClear exclusion zone established; public excluded; crew knows DZ boundaries
WeatherWind below crew's operational threshold; no lightning within 10 km; visibility adequate
CrewAll crew members briefed, roles assigned, PPE correct for task
CommunicationSignals confirmed and understood; noise mitigation in place if needed
EmergencyFirst aid kit accessible; emergency plan confirmed; 911 route known

🛑 NO-GO Conditions (Mandatory Stop)

ConditionAction
Energized lines within 3 m of work zoneStop all work; contact utility; do not resume until de-energized or line-clearance crew on site
Active root plate failure risk (heaving, cracking)Do not climb; assess with qualified arborist; consider emergency removal techniques from ground
Public unable to be excluded from DZDelay start until DZ can be controlled; or replan to eliminate DZ toward public
Any crew member has not been briefedHalt; complete briefing for all personnel before proceeding
Climber not fit for duty (fatigue, impairment, injury)Do not climb; reallocate work or reschedule
Wind conditions exceeding crew thresholdMonitor; delay or reschedule; do not accept pressure to proceed
Uncontrolled hazard that cannot be mitigatedReschedule; document; communicate to client in writing

The No-Go authority belongs to every crew member. ANSI Z133 §4 supports any worker's right to refuse work they believe is unsafe. A crew member calling a no-go is doing their job. The response from the lead arborist must be to address the concern — not to pressure the worker to proceed.

📝 Documentation Best Practice

While ANSI Z133 mandates the inspection and meeting, it does not specify written documentation. However, maintaining a brief written record serves three purposes:

  • WorkSafeBC compliance — demonstrates due diligence if an incident occurs and is investigated
  • Client communication — if a no-go condition means rescheduling, a written note protects the company
  • Training tool — newer crew members seeing written inspections develop their own inspection habits

A field inspection form (even a simple photo + voice memo) is more than most crews do and significantly better than nothing. Ask your lead arborist about Microbe Tree's current documentation protocol.
