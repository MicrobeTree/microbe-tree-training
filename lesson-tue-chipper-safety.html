Disc Chipper vs Drum Chipper — Know Your Machine

Bandit manufactures both disc and drum chippers. Understanding the mechanical differences matters for safe operation, kickback risk, and what material each handles best.

FeatureDisc Chipper (Bandit 15XP, 18XP, 1890XP etc.)Drum Chipper (Bandit 65, 75 series etc.)
Cutting mechanismRotating steel disc with fixed knives — chip is sliced off as material contacts the disc faceRotating drum with helical knives — material is fed perpendicular to drum rotation
Chip qualityMore uniform chip size; preferred for biomass/fuel chip specSlightly more variable; excellent for large diameter material
Kickback potentialLower kickback risk — slicing action with discHigher kickback risk with some large-diameter or forked material — drum can grab and eject
Large diameter capacityLimited by disc face diameter; typically 6"–18" capacityHandles large, irregular material well; 12"–24"+ capacity
Typical applicationMost Okanagan tree care brush/limb workLarger production chipping, land clearing
📋 Field Note — Okanagan

Bandit disc chippers are the workhorse of most Okanagan tree care operations. The 12" and 15" disc machines are common on residential jobs. Know your specific machine's disc diameter and knife count — Bandit 1590XP has a 30" disc with 2 knives; the 18XP runs a larger disc with 3. Knife count affects chip thickness and the feel of feeding. When something "sounds different" during chipping, stop and check — disc imbalance or a cracked knife makes itself known acoustically before it fails catastrophically.

Infeed Zone Rules — ANSI Z133 §8.4 / WorkSafeBC OHS Reg. §26.20

⚠ The infeed zone is the most dangerous area on the job site. A chipper drum or disc can pull a person in faster than any reflex can respond. There is no recovery from contact with the knives.

Mandatory Infeed Zone Rules (ANSI Z133:2017 §8.4)

  • Never feed material while standing in front of the infeed chute — always feed from the side, maintaining a 90° offset from the feed direction. The "side-feed" position keeps you out of the pull zone
  • Feed brush and limbs butt-end first whenever practical — the chipper grabs butt-end material more smoothly and reduces the chance of hang-ups that cause sudden jolts
  • Maintain a minimum 6-foot clearance from the infeed opening when the machine is running and you are not actively feeding — this applies to ground crew observing and bystanders
  • Never reach into the infeed chute to clear a jam — shut the machine down and wait for all rotation to stop before clearing blockages. Per ANSI Z133, the engine must be off and the disc/drum fully stopped
  • Long material creates a "lever" hazard — material longer than approximately 6 feet being pushed into the chipper can pivot and strike the operator. Feed long brush butt-first and let the machine pull it; never push from behind on long pieces
  • If material stalls or the machine bogs, engage the reverse/retract control (the green/yellow paddle or bar at infeed) to back material out — never pull on stuck material by hand while the machine is running
  • No loose clothing, drawstrings, or gloves hanging loose near the infeed — the chipper does not distinguish between a branch and a sleeve

Infeed Control Bar (Bandit) — Function

Bandit chippers use a bar or paddle across the top of the infeed opening. This is the feed control / emergency stop bar:

  • Upper position (pulled toward operator) → engages automatic feed (material is drawn in by hydraulic feed rollers)
  • Middle / neutral position → feed rollers disengage; chipping rotor continues running
  • Lower position (pushed away from operator) → reverses feed rollers to back material out of infeed
📋 Field Note — Okanagan

In tight residential yards in West Kelowna, the pressure to chip fast and move is real — but tight job sites also mean you're more likely to have forked brush, wet material, and people moving through the chip throw zone. Before you start chipping on any site, identify your feed zone offset position and make sure every crew member knows it. The WorkSafeBC fatality database has multiple chipper incidents where crew fed material while standing in front of the infeed — always from the side, every single time.

Emergency Stop Procedures — Bandit Chippers

If a Person Makes Contact with the Infeed or Chipper Components

This is a life-safety emergency. Act without hesitation.
  • Step 1: Hit the emergency stop / kill switch — a large red mushroom-head button located on the control panel and/or at the rear of the machine. All Bandit machines since 2005 have at minimum one emergency stop. Newer models have additional stops at infeed and rear
  • Step 2: Simultaneously, any crew member nearest the infeed can push the infeed control bar to the reverse position to back material out — but this is secondary to engine stop
  • Step 3: Call 911 immediately — do not delay emergency services to assess the situation
  • Step 4: Do not attempt to move the victim or extract limbs from the machine. Wait for emergency responders. Moving a crush victim from machinery can cause fatal hemorrhage upon release
  • Step 5: Secure the machine — prevent it from being restarted until emergency services and WorkSafeBC have been notified. Chipper incidents are WorkSafeBC-reportable serious injuries — report within 24 hours per OHS Regulation §172

Pre-Task Emergency Plan — Required by ANSI Z133 §4.1

Per ANSI Z133:2017, a pre-task emergency action plan must be discussed at the start of every shift or job site change. For chipper work, this means verbally confirming:

  • Location of all emergency stops on this specific machine
  • Who calls 911 and who secures the area
  • Nearest hospital with trauma care (Kelowna General for most Okanagan jobs)
  • That every crew member can operate the infeed control bar independently
📋 Field Note — Okanagan

Walk-around every chipper at the start of every day. Locate the E-stops before you need them — not while panicking. On Bandit machines, the mushroom E-stop is bright red and prominent, but on older machines it may be stiff from lack of use. Test it occasionally (with the machine safely positioned). WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation §26.15 requires that emergency controls be inspected before each use. That inspection takes 30 seconds and could be the most important thing you do all day.

PPE Requirements for Chipper Operation — ANSI Z133 §3 / WorkSafeBC OHS Reg.

Mandatory PPE — All Chipper Operations

  • Hard hat: CSA Z94.1 Class E (electrical) or ANSI Z89.1 Type II. Required whenever overhead work or falling debris risk exists — chipper work near the tree qualifies. Inspect shell and suspension before each shift
  • Eye protection: Safety glasses or goggles meeting CSA Z94.3 / ANSI Z87.1. Chip discharge and wood debris make this non-negotiable. Face shield is additional, not a substitute for safety glasses
  • Hearing protection: Required when sound levels exceed 85 dBA (8-hour TWA). Chipper noise commonly exceeds 95–105 dBA at the infeed position. Foam earplugs (NRR 29+) or earmuffs rated for the exposure level — WorkSafeBC OHS Reg. §7.98
  • High-visibility vest or jacket: Required whenever working within proximity of vehicle traffic (ANSI 107 Class 2 minimum for roadside) and strongly recommended on any job site for crew visibility
  • Work gloves: Leather palm at minimum for brush handling. Cut-resistant gloves appropriate for chainsaw chip handling. Critically: gloves must fit snugly — loose gloves near the infeed can be snagged
  • Leg protection (chainsaw chaps): Required for any person using a chainsaw in the vicinity of the chipper. Not required for chipper operation alone, but standard Microbe Tree practice is chaps-on until the saw is secured
  • Steel-toed boots: CSA Z195 green patch (protective toe cap) minimum. Log and branch handling on site requires foot protection

What NOT to Wear Around the Chipper

Never wear: loose drawstring hoods, flapping jacket sleeves, dangling lanyards or badge holders, scarves, open-toed footwear, or unsecured hair near the infeed zone. The chipper will not give you time to react.
📋 Field Note — Okanagan

Hearing damage from chipper exposure is cumulative and irreversible. A 10-minute exposure at 100 dBA without protection equals significant noise dose. Many experienced arborists have measurable high-frequency hearing loss from years of inadequate hearing protection. Foam earplugs have an NRR of 29 dB — worn correctly, they reduce 100 dBA to approximately 85 dBA, right at the WorkSafeBC limit. Double-up (plugs under earmuffs) for extended chipping shifts. Your hearing at 55 depends on the choices you make at 25.

ANSI Z133 Chipper Quick Reference — 2017 Edition

ANSI Z133 "Safety Requirements for Arboricultural Operations" is the primary US/Canadian industry standard for tree work safety. Section 8 covers chipping operations. BC WorkSafeBC OHS Regulation Part 26 (Forestry) and Part 4 (General HV&V) also apply. Where they conflict, the more stringent requirement governs.

ANSI Z133 SectionRequirement
§3 — PPEHard hat, eye protection, hearing protection, appropriate clothing — all mandatory for chipper operation
§4.1 — Pre-Task PlanDiscuss emergency procedures, hazards, escape routes, and equipment operation at start of each work day
§8.4.1 — Infeed positionOperators shall feed brush from the side — not standing in front of the infeed throat
§8.4.2 — Clearing jamsEngine off, disc/drum fully stopped before clearing any blockage
§8.4.3 — Loose clothingProhibits loose garments or jewelry near moving parts of the chipper
§8.4.4 — Minimum crewA minimum of two workers shall be present when operating a chipper
§8.4.5 — Tow vehicleChipper shall be secured from movement during operation (wheel chocks, tow vehicle in park)
§8.5 — Chip throw zoneNo persons in the chip throw/discharge zone during operation

WorkSafeBC Specifics (OHS Regulation Part 26)

  • §26.15: All dangerous machine parts must be guarded or workers excluded from danger zones
  • §26.20: Specific chipper infeed requirements — equivalent to ANSI Z133 §8.4, with BC legal force
  • §172: Serious injury reporting — chipper incidents with injury or potential for serious injury must be reported to WorkSafeBC within 24 hours
  • BC requires documented pre-use equipment inspection records for powered equipment — keep a log
✓ Daily chipper check: Inspect disc/drum knives for chips, cracks, or loosening. Check feed roller condition. Verify all guards are in place. Test emergency stop function. Check hydraulic fluid level. Confirm wheel chocks are aboard. Takes 5 minutes. Required by law.
