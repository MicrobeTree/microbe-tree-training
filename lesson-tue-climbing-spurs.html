Climbing Spurs & Gaffs: Inspect, Sharpen, Fit, Removals Only

Gauge checks, the plane test, how to sharpen without ruining a gaff, how they should fit, and why spurs never go on a tree we're pruning.

🦵 Our rule: spurs on removals only

  • Microbe Tree rule: spurs go on removals only, never on a live tree we're pruning. Every gaff hole is a wound.
  • The Davey Tree bulletin (published by the City of Elgin) says spiking a tree you're pruning leaves entry sites for wood-rotting fungi, and that spurs should only be used on trees that are dead or being removed, or in an emergency rescue. Monday's CODIT lesson explains why that wound matters.
  • ANSI A300 (as quoted in Maryland DNR's Tree Expert study guide): climbing spurs shall not be used when climbing and pruning trees. A300 lists narrow exceptions: emergencies, limbs more than a throw-line apart with no other way up, outer bark thick enough to protect the inner bark and cambium, and remote or rural utility rights-of-way. Our rule is stricter. If you think one of those exceptions applies, ask Anton first.

🔍 Inspect before every climb (Klein)

  • Straps and pads: no burns, cuts, broken stitches or excessive wear. Buckles close properly, aren't distorted or cracked, and the tongue doesn't bind. Rivets aren't bent, loose or missing.
  • Gaffs: no dents, gouges or scratches. Underside length longer than the minimum, 2-1/4 in (57 mm) for tree gaffs. Width, thickness and point profile checked on the manufacturer's gaff gauge.
  • Stirrup (Buckingham): if any part of the stirrup edge fits the gauge's stirrup-thickness slot, the climber is too worn to use.
  • Fails anything? Klein says to resharpen the gaffs or discard and replace them. Climbers that fail inspection are removed from service and destroyed.
  • Plane test (Klein): rest the climber gaff-down on a horizontal board and push it along with no downward pressure except its own weight. A sharp gaff buries itself within a few inches. If it slips, slides or plows a shallow groove, it isn't sharp enough.

🪚 Sharpening: hone first, file carefully

  • Buckingham: in normal use, gaffs need only honing. File only when they're nicked, damaged or badly worn, using a smooth single-cut file.
  • Klein: never grind on a wheel, because the heat alters the gaff's temper. File from heel to point. Never cross-file, because it weakens the point and can break the tip under load.
  • No needle points. Klein: keep the top ridge straight and the underside flat, rounded slightly near the tip. Buckingham: a tip that ends in a sharp point without the radius is unsafe. The radius is what drives the gaff in. Klein says a badly sharpened gaff can "cut out".
  • Re-check every sharpened gaff on the gauge and with the plane test before you climb.

🧷 Fit & use

  • Fit (Klein): adjust the shank so the sleeve sits about 1 in (25 mm) below the knee. Gaffs go on the inside of the legs, with stirrups marked L and R. Fasten the calf and ankle straps securely but comfortably.
  • Bark thickness: ponderosa pine and Douglas-fir bark is thick. WesSpur notes tree gaffs are usually 2-3/4 in or longer so they get through thick bark into the wood. Klein: when bark is over 2-1/2 in (64 mm), make sure the gaff is long enough to reach the wood. Test penetration at the base before you climb.
  • Between climbs: Klein says to take climbers off, because gaffs are damaged by rocks and by striking each other while you walk. Use gaff guards. Avoid nails and other metal in the tree.
  • WorkSafeBC 26.12.0.4(e): be secured to the tree by your climbing system before you access the tree, and stay secured until you're back on the ground. That means your lanyard or flipline goes on before the first step.
  • WorkSafeBC 26.12.0.4(f): operate a cutting tool only when tied in with a climbing line or lanyard plus a second means of securement.
  • WorkSafeBC 26.12.0.6(2): the tree-climbing system (harness, lines, lanyards) must be inspected by a qualified arborist before first use each shift.
Question 1 of 5
What is the minimum underside length for a tree-climbing gaff, according to Klein and Buckingham?
2-1/4 in on the flat underside
  • Buckingham: minimum safe gaff length for tree climbing is 2-1/4 in, measured on the flat underside. Shorter is too worn.
  • Klein: 2-1/4 in (57 mm) for tree gaffs. 1-7/16 in is the pole gaff minimum.
Question 2 of 5
How should you sharpen a gaff?
Heel to point, no grinding
  • Klein: never grind (heat alters the temper), file heel to point, never cross-file, and no needle point.
  • Buckingham: normal use needs only honing, and a point without its radius is unsafe.
Question 3 of 5
In Klein's plane test, the gaff slides along the board and plows a shallow groove. What does that mean?
A sharp gaff buries itself
  • Klein: a properly sharpened gaff buries itself within a few inches. One that slips, slides or plows a groove is not properly sharpened.
  • A badly sharpened gaff can cut out under load.

You're on spurs at the top of a removal and about to start the saw. What does WorkSafeBC 26.12.0.4(f) require?
Two points before cutting
