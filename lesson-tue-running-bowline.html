The Running Bowline: Your Rigging Choke

Choke it, load it, and know how it comes back down. When to use it, when not to, and what not to add to it.

⏱ About 10 minutes · Week 21 · Microbe Tree Inc. crew training

🪢 The short answer

The running bowline is just a bowline tied around its own standing part: a noose that chokes a limb or piece when the line is loaded. It's the everyday way to hang a rigging line on a piece you're about to cut and lower. It can be set from the ground or from a distance, it holds while it's under tension, and it comes undone easily when the load is off.

Bowline around the rope's own standing part
Choking a branch or piece in rigging; setting a top anchor
Creeps or unravels if left slack
Not retrievable once sent up the line
📐 Every knot costs rope strength. VTIO's arborist knot guide says a knot can cost as much as 60% of rope strength, and leave a tail of roughly 8× the rope diameter (about 10 cm / 4 in on 12.5 mm rope). Always Tie, Dress, Set.
📋 Field Note: Okanagan

Think of a ponderosa limb over a West Kelowna fence line or a Douglas-fir top you're lowering in pieces: you want a hitch that grabs the piece, holds while the line is tight, and lets go when you're done. That's this knot. Just plan how it comes back down before you send it up.

Question 1 of 5
A crewmate says "a running bowline is just a bowline." What is the difference?
A noose, not a fixed loop
  • VTIO: "Simply tie a bowline around its own 'standing part'."
  • Sterling Rope: it's intended to cinch up or choke against the branch or piece when loaded.
  • Animated Knots: unlike a plain noose, the bowline form won't close up and bind on the standing end; it grips the load while the rope is under tension and is easy to undo once unloaded.
Question 2 of 5
Which use of the running bowline is NOT acceptable without extra backup?
Bowlines creep without tension
  • VTIO: a bowline that is not kept under constant tension tends to creep, distort and even unravel, so a plain bowline is NOT acceptable as a lifeline attachment knot. If used for climbing it needs to be backed up.
  • Samson Rope: the running bowline's applications are choking a line around a branch in rigging and setting a top anchor point in SRS.
  • Treestuff: a plain bowline as a basal anchor for a climbing rope gets a Yosemite finish or a stopper knot.
📋 ISA exam

Safe Work Practices (15%) covers knots or rope tools which provide safe entry and activity in the tree. Know that a knot's job and its security (backups, tail, tension) go together.

Question 3 of 5
You've tied a running bowline on a heavy piece and your helper wants to add three half hitches "for safety." What's the right answer?
Extras can reduce strength
  • Sterling Rope: "the Running Bowline actually has more strength without any marls or half-hitches; and the addition of them actually decreases strength."
  • Use them only where there's a concern about the piece's orientation or that the branch might slip free. Slick-barked species may need extra turns or "tweaks".
  • For contrast, Sterling says a clove hitch can "roll out" and must be backed up with two half hitches.
Question 4 of 5
You've thrown a rope over a high limb and tied the running bowline to choke a top anchor 12 m up. How do you plan for getting it back down?
Plan the retrieval before you send it
  • Samson Rope: "Once it is sent running up the line it is not retrievable."
  • Samson's long tail running bowline makes it retrievable, but both legs (tail and working line) need to be isolated with no obstruction.
  • Animated Knots: advance planning gives options: a light retrieval line on the bowline, or a very long tail.
  • Treestuff: "you may not be able to retrieve it if it goes too high."
Question 5 of 5
Before you load a running bowline on a rigging line, what's your routine?
TDS, every time
  • VTIO's rules of knot tying: Tie, Dress (align all the parts), Set (tighten ready for use). If you can't recognize the knot you tied, you probably didn't tie it correctly.
  • Tail: roughly 8× the rope diameter, e.g. no shorter than 10 cm (4 in) on 12.5 mm rope.
  • Treestuff: always double check your knots and TDS; don't use a knot you aren't comfortable with when there's any risk.
📋 Field Note: Microbe Tree Crew

Sterling's alternative: the Halter hitch is also a choking hitch that's fairly easy to untie, but they note there's no strength-loss data for it. Know the running bowline first.
