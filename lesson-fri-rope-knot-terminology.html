Rope & Knot Terminology: Tie It From Words Alone

The shared vocabulary that lets one person talk another through any knot, plus a worked bowline and today's tailgate drill.

⏱ About 10 minutes · Week 22 · Microbe Tree Inc. crew training

🗣️ The short answer

If you can't say how to tie a knot, you don't really own it. On a job, you might be guiding a groundie through a knot from 15 m up with a saw idling, or checking a new hire's knot over the radio. The goal today is to tie any knot we use from words alone. That only works if everyone means the same thing by bight, loop and standing part.

📐 Tie, Dress, Set (VTIO). If you can't recognize the knot you just tied, you probably didn't tie it correctly.

📖 The words

TermMeaning
Working endThe end you're tying with: the part doing the knotting (Animated Knots, Useful Knots, VTIO).
Standing partThe rope between the knot and the standing end. It's the long part not being knotted, and often the part that takes the load (Animated Knots).
Standing endThe far end of the long part, away from the knot (Animated Knots).
Running end ⚠️Sources disagree. Useful Knots says it's the working end. VTIO's arborist guide says it's the end you're not tying with. Don't use this word in a word-only instruction unless you've both agreed what it means.
TailThe short, loose end left after the knot is tied (Animated Knots). Leave enough: VTIO says roughly 8× the rope diameter.
BightRope folded back so the two parts lie alongside each other, a U shape that doesn't cross itself. A bight is not a loop (Animated Knots, Useful Knots).
LoopA partial or full circle where the rope crosses itself (Animated Knots, Useful Knots).
Crossing turnAnother name for a loop where the rope crosses over itself. Useful Knots: when the legs cross to form a loop, the rope has "taken a turn". In a word-only instruction, always say which part goes on top.
TurnOne pass of the rope around or through an object (Animated Knots).
Round turnTwo passes, so the rope completely encircles the object and comes back alongside the standing part (Animated Knots, Useful Knots).
DressArrange the parts of the knot neatly so it's secure and strong (Animated Knots, VTIO).
SetTighten the dressed knot so it's ready for use (Useful Knots, VTIO).
Hitch / BendA hitch attaches rope to an object. A bend joins two ropes (Animated Knots).

🎯 Rules for word-only instructions

  • Name the part, then the move, then the direction: "Take the working end… up through the loop… from underneath."
  • Over / under / behind / through beat "this way" and "like that". Avoid "clockwise", since it flips depending on where you're standing.
  • For every loop, say which part crosses on top.
  • Finish with the checks: dress it, set it, check the tail, and check it looks right.

🪢 Worked example: a bowline in words only

Based on Animated Knots' bowline steps (form a small loop, pass the end through as though making an overhand knot, continue around the standing end, back through the small loop), spelled out with today's terms:

  1. "Hold the rope with the standing part going away from you. Measure off enough working end for the size of eye you want, plus a tail."
  2. "In the standing part, make a small loop so the rope leading to the working end crosses over the top of the standing part."
  3. "Bring the working end up through that small loop from underneath. That leaves a big eye hanging below."
  4. "Take the working end behind the standing part, above the small loop."
  5. "Bring the working end back down through the small loop, the same way it came up, so it lies alongside itself."
  6. "Dress it: the tail should finish on the inside of the eye." (VTIO; Animated Knots notes that if the end goes around the other way, you get a "left-handed" bowline.)
  7. "Set it: hold the tail and the leg of the eye beside it together, and pull the standing part firmly. Check the tail is long enough."
⚠️ Bowline limitsAnimated Knots: a bowline can shake loose when unloaded and needs a safety knot or Yosemite tie-off for climbing. VTIO: an unbacked bowline is not acceptable as a lifeline attachment knot.

🤝 Tailgate drill: Say It, Tie It (about 10 min)

  1. Pair up. The Describer stands back-to-back with the Tier, with no pointing and no demonstrating.
  2. The Describer talks the Tier through a knot we use (bowline, running bowline, clove hitch, figure-8), using only today's terms.
  3. The Tier does exactly what they hear, not what they think was meant.
  4. Check together: compare it with a known-good knot, then dress, set and check the tail.
  5. If it's wrong, find the word that caused it. Was it a vague direction, "loop" when you meant "bight", or not saying what crossed on top?
  6. Swap roles, and pick a different knot.
📋 Field Note: Microbe Tree Crew

Practice knots only, on a practice rope at ground level. Nothing goes into a life-support or rigging system from this drill without the usual check by a qualified climber.

Question 1 of 5
What is the difference between a bight and a loop?
Crossing is the difference
  • Animated Knots: the term "bight" does not imply a loop and doesn't mean the same thing.
  • Useful Knots: a bight is a length of rope that does not cross itself. A loop is formed by passing the working end over itself.
Question 2 of 5
You're telling a groundie how to tie a knot by radio and say "take the running end around the limb." Why is that risky?
Use words with one meaning
  • Useful Knots: running end = the end used to tie the knot (the working end).
  • VTIO: the running end is the end you are not tying with.
  • Same word, opposite meanings. In a word-only instruction, pick terms that everyone agrees on.
Question 3 of 5
What's a round turn?
One turn vs round turn
  • Animated Knots: a turn is one pass of the rope round or through an object. A round turn is two passes, completely encircling it.
  • Useful Knots: in a round turn, the running end comes back along the standing part.
Question 4 of 5
"Dress" and "set": which is which?
Tie, Dress, Set
  • Animated Knots: dressing = arranging the components of the knot for security and/or strength.
  • Useful Knots: setting = tightening it. Improper setting can make a knot underperform.
  • VTIO: Tie, Dress, Set, every time.

Question 5 of 5
In a correctly tied standard bowline, where does the tail finish?
Tail inside
  • VTIO: tied correctly, the working end finishes on the inside of the loop.
  • Animated Knots: if the end is passed the opposite way around the standing end, you get a left-handed bowline, with the tail outside.
