📋 Quick Reference — Diplodia Tip Blight
Causal agent
Sphaeropsis sapinea (Diplodia pinea) — fungal pathogen, Botryosphaeriaceae. Also now called Diplodia sapinea.
Hosts in BC Interior
Primary host: ponderosa pine (Pinus ponderosa). Also Austrian pine, Scots pine, lodgepole pine. NOT significant on Douglas-fir or spruce.
Diagnostic signs
New shoots die back in spring; needles remain attached, turn red-brown; black pycnidia at needle bases and on cone scales; resin at shoot base.
When symptoms appear
April–June for tip blight (infection during bud break). May appear later in summer as “bluing” of shoots following tip kill.
Conditions favouring
Wet spring during bud break; stressed trees (drought, root damage, construction injury, heavy pruning). Young trees 5–20 years most severely affected.
Key point
Stress disease. Healthy, vigorous pines resist. Weakened trees are overwhelmed. Control = tree vigor + strategic pruning of infected material.
