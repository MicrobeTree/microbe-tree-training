Photosynthesis

From sunlight to sugar — and why Okanagan summers shut the whole thing down at 2pm.

� Quick Reference — The Two Stages

☀️ Light Reactions

  • Location: thylakoid membranes
  • Input: light energy + H₂O
  • H₂O split → O₂ released (photolysis)
  • Produces: ATP + NADPH
  • PS II (P680) absorbs light first
  • PS I (P700) re-energizes electrons

🌿 Calvin Cycle

  • Location: stroma (chloroplast fluid)
  • Input: CO₂ + ATP + NADPH
  • RuBisCO fixes CO₂ to RuBP
  • Produces: G3P → glucose
  • 3 CO₂ → 1 G3P molecule
  • Does NOT require direct light
Key Pigments
Chlorophyll a — primary, absorbs red + blue-violet
Chlorophyll b — accessory, broadens light harvesting
Carotenoids — photoprotection + accessory
📍 Okanagan context: At 35–40°C on a south-facing slope in July, stomata begin closing to limit water loss long before heat denatures any enzymes. CO₂ entry drops. Photosynthesis slows or stops entirely during peak afternoon heat. Trees in the Okanagan often have a bimodal photosynthesis pattern — peaks in the cooler morning and late afternoon, with a midday depression during peak VPD (vapour pressure deficit).
