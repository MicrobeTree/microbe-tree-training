Crabapple (Malus spp.): Small Apples, Big Fire Blight Risk

How to ID it, how it grows, why fire blight is the disease that matters here, and why black knot isn't one of its problems.

🍎 The short answer

Crabapple = Malus, in the rose family (Rosaceae). The only official line between a crabapple and an apple is fruit size: Iowa State Extension says crabapples have fruit 2 inches or less across; bigger than that is an apple. In West Kelowna yards, that means the same disease, fire blight, that orchardists fight all around us.

🔎 ID & structure

  • Leaves are alternate, simple and toothed. Fruit is a small apple in yellow, orange or red (Iowa State). Many newer cultivars hold their fruit into winter, which helps with winter ID.
  • Forms vary by cultivar: columnar (e.g. 'Centurion', 'Red Barron'), weeping ('Louisa'), and compact or rounded ('Coralburst', 'Lollipop'), per CSU's cultivar table. Know the form before you prune it.
  • Suckers and water sprouts: CSU says crabapples usually send up suckers next to the trunk, which need regular trimming. Penn State: remove root suckers and water sprouts while they're small, as part of fire-blight management.
  • Site: CSU calls crabapples fairly drought tolerant. That's a plus in our dry interior.

🔥 Fire blight (Erwinia amylovora)

  • Hosts: BC's fire blight factsheet lists about 200 landscape species, including crab apple, hawthorn, mountain ash and Bradford pear. Apple and pear are the main crops of concern.
  • Symptoms (Penn State): dead blossoms that stay attached, twig tips that turn brown or black and curl into a shepherd's crook, dead leaves that hang on, and sunken cankers that can ooze in wet spring weather.
  • How it spreads (BC): the bacterium overwinters in cankers, and in spring the ooze is carried to blossoms by wind, rain and insects. It enters through wounds and natural openings. Hail can set off bad outbreaks. Bacteria grow minimally below 10°C and rapidly above 18°C, peaking around 27°C.
  • Dormant pruning (now): cut out overwintering cankers. BC: cuts 15–30 cm below the canker margins. Penn State: prune when the weather is dry during dormancy and disinfect tools frequently.
  • In season: BC says to remove strikes at least 30 cm below visible disease, disinfect pruners between cuts, avoid pruning in wet weather or when storms are forecast within 24 hours, and avoid heavy summer pruning, which pushes susceptible late growth.
⚖️ Sources disagree on the cut distance: Penn State says at least 4 in (~10 cm) below the canker, Utah State says 12 in, and BC says 15–30 cm when dormant and 30 cm in summer. We're in BC: use the BC numbers.
🚫 Black knot is NOT a crabapple diseaseBlack knot (Apiosporina morbosa) attacks stone fruits in the genus Prunus (WVU Extension). It's very common on plums and wild cherries such as chokecherry. If a client points to black, warty galls, check the host first. On a crabapple, don't call it black knot.

🍂 Other crabapple problems to know

  • CSU lists fire blight, powdery mildew, apple scab and juniper-apple rust as the main crabapple diseases. Apple scab shows as leaf blotches, yellowing and early leaf drop.
  • Apple is a host of oystershell scale, Thursday's lesson. Check crabapple bark for it while you're pruning.

🌸 Cultivars: resistance ratings conflict

  • CSU rates 'Adams', 'Centurion', 'Coralburst', 'David', 'Louisa', 'Prairifire' and 'Robinson' resistant to fire blight and apple scab.
  • 'Dolgo': BC's factsheet lists it as resistant, Utah State rates its fire-blight resistance good, but CSU and Wisconsin rate it susceptible. Don't promise a client it won't blight.
  • 'Snowdrift': BC says susceptible and Wisconsin says very susceptible. Here the sources agree.
📋 Field Note: Okanagan

We work surrounded by apple and pear orchards, and the fire blight bacterium doesn't care whether a Malus is a backyard ornamental or a commercial tree. Dormant season, dry day, disinfect between trees, cut well below cankers, and bag or haul blighted wood. Don't leave it in the yard.

Question 1 of 5
What officially separates a crabapple from an apple?
It's all about fruit size
  • Iowa State Extension: apples and crabapples are differentiated strictly on the size of their fruit. Crabapples have fruit 2 inches or less in diameter.
Question 2 of 5
A client says their crabapple has black knot. What's your first thought?
Check the host
  • WVU Extension: black knot (Apiosporina morbosa) attacks stone fruit species in the genus Prunus, and it's very common on plums and wild cherries.
  • Crabapple is Malus, not Prunus. Get the ID right before you diagnose.
Question 3 of 5
You're dormant-pruning a crabapple with fire blight cankers in West Kelowna. Which plan matches BC guidance?
Get below it, keep it dry, keep it clean
  • BC factsheet: cut overwintering cankers out during the dormant season, with cuts 15–30 cm below the canker margins.
  • Penn State: prune during dormancy when the weather is dry, and disinfect pruning tools frequently.
  • Cankers left in the tree are next spring's inoculum.
Question 4 of 5
Above roughly what temperature does the fire blight bacterium start growing rapidly, according to BC?
Warm, wet bloom = danger
  • BC: growth is minimal below 10°C, slow from 10–18°C, increases rapidly above 18°C, and peaks at 27°C. It can decline above 35°C.
  • Bloom on warm, humid days plus a wetting event (rain, dew, overhead irrigation) is the classic infection setup.

Question 5 of 5
A client wants a crabapple that "won't get fire blight" and asks about 'Dolgo'. What's the honest answer?
Resistance is a rating, not a promise
  • BC factsheet: 'Dolgo' resistant. Utah State: good. CSU: S (susceptible) for fire blight and scab. Wisconsin: susceptible.
  • CSU rates 'Adams', 'Centurion', 'David', 'Louisa', 'Prairifire' and 'Robinson' resistant to both fire blight and scab.
