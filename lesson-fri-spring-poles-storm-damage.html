Spring Poles & Storm-Damaged Wood

Compression vs tension, how to find the release spot, the relief cuts that bleed off the energy, and the other hazards in storm debris.

🌪️ After the storm: everything is loaded

  • Spring poles are saplings or limbs bent and held down by a fallen tree or debris. USFS: they can store a tremendous amount of energy. Alabama Extension: the bend works like a spring and can lash out or snap with extreme force when it's cut.
  • UGA Extension: a spring pole strike can hit you with the trunk, the limb or the saw. It happens mostly during limbing and bucking, but it can happen up in the canopy with storm damage too.
  • The first rule: USFS and the PCTA sawyer guide both say to cut a spring pole only when necessary. The safest spring pole is one you avoid.

⚖️ Compression vs tension

  • On any bent stem, the inside of the bend is in compression (fibres squeezed) and the outside is in tension (fibres stretched). USFS: the side under compression is always the inside of the bend.
  • UGA: never cut on the tension side. Alabama Extension: cutting into the outside edge gives a quick, uncontrolled, explosive release.
  • Logs on the ground (Husqvarna): work out whether the compression is on the top or the bottom. Start with a cut about one-third of the way through from the compression side (where you expect the bar to pinch), then finish from the opposite side. A log supported at both ends has compression on top. Watch how the log reacts, because you may have misjudged it.
  • Husqvarna: stand off to the side of the cut, because the trunk can jump up or throw back. On a slope, never stand downhill of the log.

🪚 Relief cuts on a spring pole

  • Find the spot (USFS, UGA, PCTA): draw a vertical line up from the base and a horizontal line out from the top of the bend. From where they meet, a 45° line back to the pole marks where to cut.
  • Make the cuts on the inside of the bend. USFS (Saws That Sing): a series of small cuts on the compression side. Cut until the pole moves or the saw starts to bind, pull out, and place the next cut a little way along. The energy bleeds off and the final break is less violent.
  • Shave instead of plunging. UGA and Alabama Extension describe shave cuts: thin layers taken off the compression side until the pole relaxes. Alabama notes a straight plunge into compression wood will pinch the saw.
  • Finish last. PCTA: don't cut all the way through. The fibres on the tension side still hold the pieces until you cut them once the tension is gone. USFS 2006 guide: make the release cut on the piece that will fall to the ground, and stand clear of its path.
  • One sawyer only. USFS: never use two sawyers on a spring pole. Plan your escape route before the first cut.
⚠️ Storm-damaged wood: the other hazardsLook up first. USFS: don't cut under widow-makers or loose debris; remove the hazard or move. Alabama Extension: small limbs keep falling for days and weeks after a storm, so wear a hard hat and don't work under snags or hung limbs. Uprooted trees (Husqvarna): never cut a large windfall at the butt end first, because the tree can kick over toward you. Bent trunks under high tension: Husqvarna says to stand on the inside of the arc and cut a little at a time from the pressure side, watching the bar nose for kickback.
  • WorkSafeBC 26.2.1: for work outside forestry operations and tree-climbing activities, work that involves bucking or manually falling trees (and pruning or taking trees down in pieces) must be planned. The plan identifies the hazards and how they'll be eliminated or minimized, is communicated to each worker before work starts, and is documented. If conditions change, the plan is amended and communicated before the change is carried out.

Question 1 of 5
Which side of a spring pole do you cut first?
Inside of the bend
  • USFS: make a series of small cuts on the side under compression, which is always the inside of the bend.
  • UGA: never cut on the tension side.
Question 2 of 5
How do USFS, UGA and PCTA find the best place to release a spring pole?
The 45° method
  • USFS (Saws That Sing) and PCTA: vertical line from the base, horizontal line from the top of the bend, 45° back from the intersection.
  • USFS: avoid severing bent limbs at the bole. Cut at the calculated spot.
Question 3 of 5
A storm-felled trunk is supported at both ends with its middle off the ground. Where is the compression, and which cut goes first?
Supported both ends = pressure on top
  • Husqvarna: if a trunk has ground contact at both ends, its upper surface is in compression.
  • Basic technique: start about 1/3 through from the compression side, then meet it from the opposite side.
Question 4 of 5
You're cleaning up a large uprooted windfall. Why does Husqvarna say not to cut it at the butt end first?
Root plates move
  • Husqvarna: windfalls with large uprooted trees must never be cut at the butt end first, because these trees could kick over in your direction.

Question 5 of 5
Overnight wind changed the site before a planned bucking job. What does WorkSafeBC 26.2.1 expect?
Change = amend and re-brief
  • WorkSafeBC 26.2.1(3): planning must identify hazards and controls, be communicated before work starts, and be documented.
