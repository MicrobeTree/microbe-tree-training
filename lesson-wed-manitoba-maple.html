Manitoba Maple (Box Elder, Acer negundo)

How to spot it in winter, why its wood breaks, the included-bark unions to watch for, and what happens after a heavy cut.

🍁 Manitoba maple at a glance

Manitoba maple, box elder, Acer negundo
Exotic; "probably naturalized" (E-Flora BC)
Spreading tree up to 20 m
Brittle wood, breakage, sprouting
  • Okanagan context: E-Flora BC lists it as exotic in BC, "rare (sometimes garden escape) in S and NE BC". Its BC ecological records include the IDF (Interior Douglas-fir) and PP (Ponderosa Pine) zones, the dry interior zones. E-Flora gives its range east of BC as southeast Alberta to western Ontario.
  • Drought tolerant once established (USDA FEIS). That's why it survives on hot, dry Okanagan lots where other trees struggle.

🔎 ID, including in winter

  • Leaves: opposite and pinnately compound with 3–7 (sometimes 9) leaflets (E-Flora). FEIS: the leaflets are shallowly lobed or coarsely toothed.
  • Twigs: FEIS describes long, smooth, green annual shoots. UF lists current-year twigs as green to brown.
  • Male and female trees. Boxelder is dioecious (FEIS, E-Flora). Female trees carry paired winged samaras in a V, 2.5–3.8 cm long (E-Flora). They hang in long chains and stay on the tree well into winter (FEIS), so they're the easiest November ID clue.
  • Boxelder bugs: USFS Silvics says the nymphs feed mainly on female trees. The trees aren't badly damaged, but the bugs invade houses in large numbers as cold weather arrives. Expect the client to ask about them.
⚠️ Weak wood: expect breakageUF/IFAS (Gilman & Watson) lists brittle, weak wood, trunk decay and a short life, and rates it susceptible to breakage. FEIS: at maturity, brittle trunks and limbs shatter. USFS Silvics: ice and wind damage is common in older trees, and the thin bark makes it easy to damage mechanically (mowers and trimmers).

🍴 Included bark

  • USDA Forest Service (How to Prune Trees): narrow, V-shaped unions often form included bark, a wedge of inward-rolled bark that prevents strong attachment and often causes a crack below the union. Codominant stems of about the same size from the same point often form included bark.
  • UF says boxelder needs pruning for strong structure. On multi-stemmed Manitoba maples, look for the inrolled bark seam and any crack running down from it.

🌱 Sprouting after heavy cuts

  • USFS Silvics: stump and root sprouts are common from young, vigorous trees. FEIS: it sprouts from exposed roots, the root crown or the stump after top-killing damage, and old trunks often throw out clusters of sprouts.
  • USDA How to Prune Trees: topping and cutting between nodes invariably produce epicormic sprouts that are weakly attached, and eventually they're held up by a decaying branch. To limit sprouts, remove no more than one-quarter of the live crown at a time, and cut back to a lateral at least one-third the diameter of the stem.
  • On a Manitoba maple that's already been topped, those sprout clusters over old cuts are weak attachments over decay. Treat them as a risk, not as new structure.
📋 Field Note: the backyard Manitoba maple

A three-stemmed Manitoba maple hangs over a carport. It was topped years ago and now has a broom of sprouts on each stub, with an inrolled bark seam between two stems. On the quote, write down the included-bark union, the sprout clusters over old heading cuts and any visible decay. Recommend structural reduction over several years (no more than 1/4 of the crown at a time) or removal. If it's a removal, tell the client to expect stump and root sprouts. Any chemical stump or sprout treatment is Anton's call, so don't promise one on site.

Question 2 of 5
It's November and the leaves are down. What's an easy clue that you're looking at a female boxelder?
Samaras hang on into winter
  • FEIS: the samaras hang in long chains, mature in autumn and remain on the tree well into the winter.
  • It's dioecious, so only female trees carry them. E-Flora: winged pairs in a V shape.
Question 3 of 5
Why do sources flag Manitoba maple for breakage?
Brittle wood
  • UF/IFAS: brittle, weak wood, susceptible to breakage.
  • USFS Silvics: ice and wind damage common in older trees. FEIS: brittle trunks and limbs shatter at maturity.

Question 4 of 5
A Manitoba maple was topped years ago. What does USDA How to Prune Trees say about the regrowth?
Weak sprouts on decay
  • USDA: topping and tipping invariably result in epicormic sprouts, which are weakly attached and eventually supported by a decaying branch.
  • Boxelder also sprouts readily from stumps, roots and old trunks (Silvics, FEIS).
Question 5 of 5
Two same-sized stems split from one point with a seam of inrolled bark between them. What is it, and why does it matter?
Included bark = weak union
  • USDA How to Prune Trees: included bark is a wedge of inward-rolled bark that prevents strong attachment, often causing a crack below the union.
  • Codominant stems of about the same size from the same position often form it.
