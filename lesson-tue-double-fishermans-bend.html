Double Fisherman's Bend: Closing Your Prusik Loop

Two strangle knots, seated tight. How to tie it, how long to leave the tails, how it creeps, and how to inspect it before you leave the ground.

🪢 The short answer

The double fisherman's bend (also called the grapevine) joins two rope ends, or the two ends of one cord, with two strangle knots, each tied around the other rope (Animated Knots). It's how a length of cord becomes a Prusik loop. VTIO calls it a very secure life-support knot. If it's on your climbing system, your life hangs on how well you tied and checked it.

2 strangle knots, each around the other's standing part
Closing a Prusik / friction-hitch loop
2–3 in / 5–7 cm (CMC)
Can jam solid after loading

🔧 How to tie it (Animated Knots' steps)

  1. Overlap the two ends, pointing in opposite directions.
  2. Take one end and wrap it two full turns around both ropes, working back toward itself.
  3. Pass that end back through those two turns and pull it snug. That's the first strangle knot.
  4. Do the same with the other end in the opposite direction: two full turns around both, then back through.
  5. Pull the standing parts so the two knots slide together and seat against each other.
📐 Tie, Dress, Set (VTIO): tie it, dress it so the wraps lie neat and parallel with nothing crossed over, then set it hard by pulling all four strands. If you don't recognize the knot you just tied, it probably isn't right.

✂️ Tails

  • CMC (rescue equipment maker): leave at least 6 in (15 cm) when joining two ropes, but on a Prusik loop, 2–3 in (5–7 cm) keeps the tails from interfering with the hitch.
  • VTIO: its general rule is a tail of about 8× rope diameter. The double fisherman's is the named exception when it's tied as a permanent knot and the tails are stitched or whipped to the rope.
  • Creep: VTIO warns that some heat-resistant hitch-cord fibres are slippery and can creep, so whip or stitch the tails once loaded. Check tail length every time you inspect.
  • Slick, high-modulus cord (Dyneema/Spectra, Kevlar/Technora): Animated Knots says to use a triple fisherman's, with three turns per side, for load-bearing.
⚠️ Life-safety rules
  • Animated Knots: "If you tie it and your life depends on it, inspect it carefully. If someone else ties it, inspect it extremely carefully."
  • A loaded double fisherman's can lock up so tightly it's effectively welded. That's fine for a permanent Prusik loop, but don't use it where you need to untie under or after load.
  • Use cord the manufacturer rates for the friction hitch and the climbing line it's going on.

🔍 Inspection checklist (every climb)

  • Two halves, each with two full wraps (three if it's a triple), and both halves seated snugly against each other.
  • Wraps neat and parallel, with nothing crossed over or loose.
  • Tails present and long enough, and not shorter than last time (creep).
  • Cord: TCIA's line-inspection list is damaged or cut strands, glazed or melted fibres, discoloured or flat spots. Check closely where the hitch grips.
  • If anything's off, retire it or retie it on the ground, never aloft.
📋 Field Note: Okanagan winter

Cold hands and stiff, frosty cord in November mornings make sloppy dressing more likely. Tie new loops indoors or in the truck, set them hard, and do a buddy check at the tailgate before anyone leaves the ground.

Question 1 of 5
What is a double fisherman's bend made of?
Two strangle knots
  • Animated Knots: two Strangle Knots (like double overhand knots) each tied round the other standing end. Also called the Grapevine Bend.
Question 2 of 5
You're tying a new Prusik loop from hitch cord. What tail length does CMC recommend?
Prusik-loop tails
  • CMC: at least 6 in (15 cm) when joining ropes, except a Prusik loop, where 2–3 in (5–7 cm) keeps the tails out of the hitch.
  • VTIO's general rule is about 8× diameter, but it names the double fisherman's as the exception when tied permanently with stitched or whipped tails.
Question 3 of 5
Your hitch cord is a slick heat-resistant fibre. What does VTIO warn about the double fisherman's on it?
Watch for creep
  • VTIO: some modern heat-resistant rope fibres are very slippery and can creep slowly, so it's advisable to whip or stitch the tail to the standing part once loaded.
  • Animated Knots: for Dyneema/Spectra or Kevlar/Technora, use a triple fisherman's for load-bearing.
Question 4 of 5
A co-worker hands you a Prusik loop they tied. Per Animated Knots, how carefully do you check it?
Someone else's knot
  • Animated Knots: "If you tie it and your life depends on it, inspect it carefully. If someone else ties it, inspect it extremely carefully."
  • It's not complicated, but it can be tied wrongly and then fail.

Question 5 of 5
When is a double fisherman's a poor choice?
It jams
  • Animated Knots: it can lock up so tightly that it is effectively welded. The Zeppelin bend joins ropes and is easier to undo.
  • VTIO: once loaded it can be very difficult to untie, so it's most commonly used in a permanent situation.
